# Campaign Journal: The Whisper Below

---

## Prologue: The Quiet Weeks

For three weeks, Gravehold knew something it had almost forgotten: peace.

The breaches were silent. The supply caravans from the outer settlements arrived on time. The Council met to discuss grain stores and aqueduct repairs rather than evacuation routes and last stands. Children played in the Lower Markets without flinching at shadows. The watchtowers reported nothing but clear skies and the ordinary migrations of ash-hawks.

It was, by any measure, the longest stretch of calm since the first Nemesis had torn through the wards a generation ago.

Some called it a blessing. Elder Councillor Sera, who had survived three sieges and buried two apprentices, called it something else.

"Gravehold doesn't get gifts," she said, during a closed session of the Council. "It gets lulls."

She was right, of course. She usually was.

## Chapter 1: The Whisper Below

### The First Disappearance

It began in the Deepvein tunnels, three miles beneath the eastern foundations. A crew of twelve miners failed to report at shift-change. When a search party descended, they found the tunnels intact — lanterns still burning, pickaxes leaning against the walls, a half-eaten meal on a crate. No blood. No struggle. No bodies.

Twelve people, gone as though they had simply stood up and walked into the dark.

The search party withdrew. The Council ordered the Deepvein tunnels sealed.

### The Second Disappearance

Four days later, a second crew vanished from the Silver Rift — a newer shaft on the opposite side of the city's under-reach. Same pattern: abandoned tools, lit lanterns, no signs of violence. The only difference was the crystals.

Every exposed vein in the Silver Rift had turned grey. Not the dull grey of exhausted ore, but a sick, lustreless grey, as though something had pulled the light out of them. Miners who had worked the Rift for years said they'd never seen anything like it. The crystals weren't just dead — they were *drained*.

The Council convened in emergency session. The Deepvein and Silver Rift were both sealed. Patrols were doubled. The Breach Mages were placed on alert.

Nothing happened for six days.

### The Survivor

On the seventh day, a man crawled out of a ventilation shaft near the Old Foundry.

His name was Kellan Voss, a third-generation miner from the Deepvein crew — the first crew, the one that had vanished eleven days earlier. He was emaciated, dehydrated, and covered in a fine grey dust that the healers could not identify. His eyes had gone the colour of tarnished silver.

Clutched in his right hand was a crystal. It was no larger than a child's fist, but it pulsed with a light that was wrong — a black radiance that seemed to drink the torchlight around it rather than reflect it. The healers tried to take it from him. He screamed until they stopped.

When he finally spoke, his voice was a rasp, barely audible over the hum of the ward-lanterns.

He said the tunnels were not empty. He said something was down there, something that had been waiting for a very long time. He said it didn't kill the others — not exactly. He said they were still down there, in a way.

His final words, before the healers sedated him, were these:

> *"It doesn't kill. It waits."*

Kellan Voss has not woken since. The black crystal still pulses in his hand. No one has been able to remove it.

The Council has issued a summons to every Breach Mage within reach of Gravehold.

Something is awake beneath the city.

And it is patient.
