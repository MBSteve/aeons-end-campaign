# Campaign Journal: The Whisper Below

---

## Prologue: The Quiet Weeks

For three weeks, Gravehold knew something it had almost forgotten: peace.

The breaches were silent. The supply caravans from the outer settlements arrived on time. The Council met to discuss grain stores and aqueduct repairs rather than evacuation routes and last stands. Children played in the Lower Markets without flinching at shadows. The watchtowers reported nothing but clear skies and the ordinary migrations of ash-hawks.

It was, by any measure, the longest stretch of calm since the first Nemesis had torn through the wards a generation ago.

Some called it a blessing. Elder Councillor Sera, who had survived three sieges and buried two apprentices, called it something else.

"Gravehold doesn't get gifts," she said, during a closed session of the Council. "It gets lulls."

She was right, of course. She usually was.

---

## Chapter 1: The Whisper Below

### The First Disappearance

It began in the Deepvein tunnels, three miles beneath the eastern foundations. A crew of twelve miners failed to report at shift-change. When a search party descended, they found the tunnels intact — lanterns still burning, pickaxes leaning against the walls, a half-eaten meal on a crate. No blood. No struggle. No bodies.

Twelve people, gone as though they had simply stood up and walked into the dark.

The search party withdrew. The Council ordered the Deepvein tunnels sealed.

### The Second Disappearance

Four days later, a second crew vanished from the Silver Rift — a newer shaft on the opposite side of the city's under-reach. Same pattern: abandoned tools, lit lanterns, no signs of violence. The only difference was the crystals.

Every exposed vein in the Silver Rift had turned grey. Not the dull grey of exhausted ore, but a sick, lustreless grey, as though something had pulled the light out of them. Miners who had worked the Rift for years said they'd never seen anything like it. The crystals weren't just dead — they were *drained*.

The Council convened in emergency session. The Deepvein and Silver Rift were both sealed. Patrols were doubled. The Breach Mages were placed on alert.

Nothing happened for six days.

### The Survivor

On the seventh day, a man crawled out of a ventilation shaft near the Old Foundry.

His name was Kellan Voss, a third-generation miner from the Deepvein crew — the first crew, the one that had vanished eleven days earlier. He was emaciated, dehydrated, and covered in a fine grey dust that the healers could not identify. His eyes had gone the colour of tarnished silver.

Clutched in his right hand was a crystal. It was no larger than a child's fist, but it pulsed with a light that was wrong — a black radiance that seemed to drink the torchlight around it rather than reflect it. The healers tried to take it from him. He screamed until they stopped.

When he finally spoke, his voice was a rasp, barely audible over the hum of the ward-lanterns.

He said the tunnels were not empty. He said something was down there, something that had been waiting for a very long time. He said it didn't kill the others — not exactly. He said they were still down there, in a way.

His final words, before the healers sedated him, were these:

> *"It doesn't kill. It waits."*

Kellan Voss has not woken since. The black crystal still pulses in his hand. No one has been able to remove it.

The Council has issued a summons to every Breach Mage within reach of Gravehold.

Something is awake beneath the city.

And it is patient.

*End of Prologue. Chapter 1 awaits the arrival of the mages.*

## Mission 1: What the Dark Attracts

### The Summons Answered

The summons went out three days ago. Breach Mages from across the scattered sanctuaries have been arriving at Gravehold's gates in ones and twos — some striding through the main causeway with entourages, others slipping in through the Warrens without announcement. The Council has billeted them across the Upper Ward, and the Spire's great hall hums with the low murmur of old rivals and stranger alliances rekindling.

Elder Councillor Sera has not slept.

She stands at the Spire's eastern balcony, watching the dawn bleed grey across the ash-fields beyond the outer wall. The healers' report on Kellan Voss sits unread on her desk. She already knows what it says: no change. The crystal still pulses. The miner still does not wake.

A runner appears on the balcony steps, breathless.

"Councilor. The eastern watchtower reports movement beyond the ash-fields. Something large. It's coming this way."

Sera does not turn. "How long?"

"An hour. Maybe less."

She closes her eyes. Of course. The lull is over.

"Summon the mages to the East Gate. All of them. We'll sort out who stays and who goes when we see what we're facing."

The runner hesitates. "Councilor… the watchtower said it's not alone. There are things moving in the ash. Smaller things. A lot of them."

Sera finally turns. Her face is calm, but her eyes are the colour of old iron.

"Then we'd better be quick."

---

The mages came.

Adelheim arrived first, striding through the main causeway with the quiet confidence of a man who had fought Nemeses before and expected to do so again. Brama followed within the hour, her satchel heavy with tinctures and poultices — she had been tending a fever outbreak in the Outer Settlements when the summons reached her, and she had simply packed her things and walked. Kadir came through the Warrens without announcement, as was her way, appearing in the Spire's great hall as though she had been there all along. Xaxos arrived last, his breaches already faintly aglow, the air around him humming with unspent charge.

By the time Elder Councillor Sera received the runner's report from the eastern watchtower, all four were already moving toward the East Gate.

### The Ash-Fields

The eastern approach to Gravehold is a wasteland of grey powder and blackened stone — the remnants of some ancient calamity that predates even the oldest Council records. Nothing grows there. Nothing lives there. Or so the watchtower captains had always believed.

The watchtower's signal-fire burned red against the dawn. Below it, spreading across the ash-fields like a stain, something was moving.

It was vast — a shape of chitin and muscle, all jagged edges and wrong angles. Rageborne. The mages recognised it from the old bestiaries: a creature of pure destructive instinct, a living storm of teeth and talons. It did not plan. It did not scheme. It simply destroyed.

The mages took their positions at the East Gate. Behind them, the outer wall rose thirty feet of fused stone and ward-laced mortar. Beyond it, the ash-fields stretched to the horizon.

Rageborne was coming.

### The Battle

Brama did not take the field.

As the other three mages moved toward the East Gate, a runner intercepted them with word from the Lower Markets — a fever had broken out among the refugees who had fled the Outer Settlements in the wake of the disappearances. Dozens were sick. The healers were overwhelmed. Brama looked at the ash-fields, then at the runner, and made her choice without hesitation. She pressed her satchel of tinctures into Adelheim's hands — "You'll need these more than I will" — and turned toward the city.

Three mages would face Rageborne.

---

Adelheim took the centre. His amethyst shard pulsed at his belt as he began the slow, methodical work of building power — drawing Aether from the breach-currents, weaving the threads of an Amplify Vision that would, given time, turn every spell into a killing stroke. He never got to finish it. Rageborne was too fast, too relentless. But Adelheim adapted. He abandoned the grand design and fed raw damage into the fight, turn after turn, a steady drumbeat of violet light that kept the creature from simply walking through them.

Xaxos burned.

His breaches flared open within the first minute, and he did not close them again. Spell after spell poured out of him — Chaos Arc, then Ignite, then something that had no name, a torrent of white-hot fury that he had been holding back since the moment he arrived at Gravehold. In one blinding sequence, he channelled sixteen distinct bolts of fire through his focused breaches, each one punching through Rageborne's chitin like a hammer through glass. The air around him shimmered with heat-haze. The ash at his feet turned to slag. For a long moment, the creature actually staggered.

Kadir watched. Kadir waited.

She had positioned herself at the flank, her emerald shard held close, her eyes tracking every movement of the creature's limbs. She was not flashy. She did not burn. She recycled her power — casting, recovering, casting again — a closed loop of green light that never seemed to diminish. When Needlemaw erupted from the ash-fields, a shrieking horror of spines and hunger, Kadir ended it before it had taken three steps. A single focused blast. The thing crumpled.

But Rageborne was not finished.

It had been building Fury throughout the battle — each Unleash adding another layer of rage, another pulse of destructive energy. The Strike, when it came, was devastating. A wave of force that shattered the fused stone of the outer wall and sent Adelheim sprawling. Gravehold's life-wards flickered. The watchtower signal-fire guttered and nearly died.

Kadir was already moving.

She had nothing left. Her breaches were dark. Her deck was empty. The emerald shard at her wrist had gone grey — drained, like the crystals in the Silver Rift. But she was still standing, and Rageborne was still breathing, and those two facts could not coexist.

She walked into the creature's reach. She did not cast a spell — there was nothing left to cast. She simply pressed her hand against the raw, exposed wound that Xaxos had torn in its hide, and she let the last flicker of her life-force flow through the emerald shard and into the creature's heart.

The light that followed was not green. It was not any colour that had a name. It was the colour of a mage giving everything.

Rageborne screamed — the first and only sound it made — and then it was still.

Kadir collapsed beside it. Her eyes were closed. Her breathing was shallow. But her hand, still pressed against the dead creature's hide, was warm.

---

### Aftermath

They carried Kadir back through the East Gate on a stretcher woven from Adelheim's cloak. Xaxos walked beside her, his breaches finally dark, his hands trembling from the effort of holding back more power than any one mage should channel. Adelheim brought up the rear, his amethyst shard cracked but still glowing faintly — a veteran's trophy.

The watchtower signal-fire burned steady again. The ash-fields were still.

Elder Councillor Sera met them at the gate. She looked at Kadir's still form, at the dead Nemesis on the horizon, at the three mages who had held the line with nothing but skill and sacrifice. She did not speak for a long moment.

Then she said, quietly: "The lull is over. But so is the first storm."

---

### The Council Debrief

That evening, the Council convened in the Spire. The mood was not celebratory — Gravehold had survived, but it had been close. The life-wards had held at ten percent. Another Strike would have breached the inner districts. Kadir was recovering in the Healer's Ward, her emerald shard slowly regaining its colour, but the healers said she would need time before she could fight again.

Brama arrived halfway through the session, her hands still stained with the work of the Lower Markets. She listened to the report in silence, then asked one question: "What about the smaller things the watchtower saw?"

Silence.

The runner had reported movement in the ash — smaller shapes, a lot of them. But during the battle, only Needlemaw had appeared. The rest had simply… vanished. Or they had never been heading for the East Gate at all.

Sera ordered the watchtowers doubled. She ordered patrols sent to the Outer Settlements. She ordered the healers to prepare for whatever came next.

And then she asked the mages a question that had no easy answer.

## Mission 2: The City Divided

### The Mage Pool

Gravehold is not a single battlefield — it is a sprawling city of districts, passages, and walls, and the mages within it cannot be everywhere at once. After the battle at the East Gate, the surviving mages are scattered across the city, each bound by the realities of distance, duty, and recovery.

**Adelheim** and **Xaxos** remain at the East Gate, overseeing the repair crews who are already at work on the shattered outer wall. They can reach the Outer Settlements within hours, or the Old Foundry within less. They cannot reach the western districts in time to respond to a crisis there — the city is too large, the Warrens too slow.

**Kadir** lies in the Healer's Ward, deep in the Upper Ward. Her emerald shard flickers weakly in the lamplight. The healers say she will recover, but not quickly. She is unavailable for the next mission — and perhaps the one after that.

**Brama** has spent the hours since the battle in the Lower Markets, tending to the fever outbreak. She is exhausted but upright, and she has made it clear she will fight if needed. She can reach the Outer Settlements or the Old Foundry, but she cannot be in two places at once.

Beyond these three, the city is full of mages who answered the Council's summons but have not yet been called to fight. They wait in the Upper Ward, in the Warrens, in the guest quarters of the Spire — restless, watching, ready.

### Council Favour

The Council does not give its trust freely. It is earned.

**Current Council Favour: 1**

Council Favour is the campaign's primary currency. It is earned through victories, heroic deeds, and completed bonus objectives. It may be spent on:

| Cost | Option |
|------|--------|
| 1 | **Side Mission** — undertake a narrative interlude (see below). Side missions do not involve an Aeon's End battle but may yield allies, relics, intelligence, or story rewards. |
| 2 | **Mage Training** — a mage of your choice gains +1 XP. |
| 2 | **Gravehold Repairs** — restore 10 to any one Gravehold attribute (Morale, Defences, Supplies, or Population). |
| 3 | **Relic Research** — unlock a relic for use in future missions. |
| 3 | **Call Reinforcements** — summon a specific mage from outside the current pool to join the active roster. |
| 4 | **District Fortification** — a chosen district gains a permanent defensive trait. |

Council Favour is never lost on a defeat — only the opportunity to spend it is delayed. The Council remembers.

### Side Mission: The Ember in the Dark

*Cost: 1 Council Favour. This is a narrative interlude — no Aeon's End battle is played. If the players choose to spend the favour, the following unfolds.*

---

A child found it.

She was scavenging in the Warrens — a girl of perhaps nine, thin and quick, one of the tunnel-children who know the deep passages better than the Council's own cartographers. She had been told to stay away from the Old Foundry. She had been told about the disappearances. She went anyway, because her family was hungry and old foundries sometimes had salvageable scrap.

What she found was not scrap.

It was a creature — no larger than a hunting dog, curled in a nest of melted slag and broken masonry. Its scales were the colour of cooling embers: deep charcoal edged with faint, flickering orange, as though a fire had recently gone out beneath its skin. Its wings were folded tight against its body, membranous and translucent, veined with something that glowed faintly in the dark. It was breathing. Barely.

The girl ran to the nearest watch-post. The watch-captain sent word to the Spire.

---

The Council's scholars identify it as a **drake** — a lesser dragon, a creature of the deep places that predate even the Nameless. This one is young, perhaps only weeks old, and it is dying. Its body temperature is dropping. Its breath comes in shallow, irregular gasps. The scholars believe it was driven from its nest by the same disturbance that woke the thing beneath the city — displaced, terrified, starving.

It needs warmth. It needs food. It needs someone to care for it.

The Council is divided. Some argue the creature is a liability — another mouth to feed, another unknown in a city already drowning in unknowns. Others argue it is an opportunity — dragons, even lesser ones, are deeply attuned to breach-energy. It might sense things the mages cannot.

Elder Councillor Sera ends the debate with a single sentence: "We are not the kind of city that lets things die in the dark."

But the Council cannot spare resources for sentiment. If the mages want to save the drake, they must spend **1 Council Favour** to requisition the necessary supplies: a heated chamber, meat from the city stores, and a scholar's time to study the creature's needs.

---

If the Council Favour is spent, the drake is saved. It is moved to a warm chamber in the Upper Ward, where it slowly recovers. It imprints on the first mage who feeds it — a bond that will grow over time.

**The drake becomes a companion.** It is too young to fight, but it is watchful, intelligent, and deeply sensitive to breach-energy. It will grow. In time, it may become something more.

- **Reward**: The drake joins the party as a **companion**. It does not affect Aeon's End gameplay directly, but it unlocks future narrative options: the drake may sense hidden threats, discover secret passages, or eventually fight alongside the mages.
- **The imprinted mage** is chosen by the players. The drake will appear in that mage's future story entries.
- **Council Favour**: -1 (spent on the drake's care).

If the Council Favour is not spent, the drake's fate is uncertain. The scholars do what they can, but without proper resources, the creature may not survive.

---

### The Two Threats

The Council has identified two immediate threats. The mages can only address one — Gravehold is stretched thin, and the city's geography means no single team can cover both fronts.

**Brama is locked in for whichever mission is chosen.** She has made her position clear: "I didn't spend the last six hours saving lives to watch the city burn now."

The fourth mage slot will be filled by a nomination from the mages who can physically reach the chosen location.

---

### Option A: The Vanished Swarm

The smaller creatures the watchtower reported — the ones that never reached the East Gate — have been sighted again. A patrol found tracks in the ash-fields, heading north-west, toward the **Outer Settlements**. The settlements are Gravehold's lifeline: farms, quarries, and the families of half the city's workforce. If something is hunting out there, it needs to be stopped before the settlements go dark.

**Mages who can reach the Outer Settlements:** Adelheim, Xaxos (AE), Brama, and any two from the Upper Ward pool.

- **Nemesis**: Carapace Queen (Tier 3, Base Game)
- **Location**: Outer Settlements (above ground, farmland and quarry)
- **Timeframe**: Urgent. If not addressed, the Outer Settlements will be **lost** — supplies drop by 20, population drops by 15.
- **Tone**: Defensive, protective. The mages are the shield.

### Option B: The Whisper's Echo

Kellan Voss stirred.

It was only for a moment — a twitch of his fingers, a flicker behind his eyelids — but the black crystal in his hand pulsed in response, and every breach-ward in the Healer's Ward flared simultaneously. The healers are frightened. The Council's scholars want to study the crystal, but they need a mage present — someone who can contain whatever might happen if the study goes wrong. And there is something else: a faint signal, detected by the breach-seismographs in the Spire, emanating from the sealed **Deepvein Tunnels**. It matches the pulse of Kellan's crystal.

**Mages who can reach the Old Foundry:** Adelheim, Xaxos (AE), Brama, and any two from the Upper Ward pool.

- **Nemesis**: Umbra Titan (Tier 3, War Eternal)
- **Location**: The Old Foundry / Deepvein approach (above ground, but at the threshold of the sealed tunnels)
- **Timeframe**: Pressing. If not addressed, the crystal's signal intensifies — threat level increases by 1, and Kellan's condition worsens.
- **Tone**: Investigative, ominous. The mages are the spear, probing the darkness.

---

### The Choice

The players must decide:

1. **Which main mission to pursue** (Option A or Option B). The other will escalate.
2. **Whether to spend 1 Council Favour** on the side mission (The Ember in the Dark) to save the drake.

The side mission is independent — it can be undertaken alongside either main mission. It represents the Council allocating resources, not the mages' time.

- **If you choose Option A**: The Outer Settlements are defended. The crystal's signal grows stronger. Something in the Deepvein tunnels takes notice.
- **If you choose Option B**: The crystal is studied. The Deepvein mystery deepens. The Outer Settlements face whatever is hunting them alone.
- **If you spend Council Favour**: The drake is saved. It bonds with a mage of your choice.
