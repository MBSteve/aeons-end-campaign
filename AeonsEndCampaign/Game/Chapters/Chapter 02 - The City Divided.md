# Chapter 02: The City Divided

> **Chapter Summary**: The Council chose to defend the Outer Settlements from the Carapace Queen's swarm. Brama, Jian, and Lash delivered a comprehensive victory — Brama struck the killing blow with twin bolts of searing light, and the mages cut the Queen down before her deepest horrors could be unleashed. But while they fought, the unchosen threat escalated: the Deepvein signal intensified, Kellan Voss's condition worsened, and the drake in the Warrens remains unsaved. In the quiet hours after, each mage wrestles with what they discovered about themselves — Brama with the weight of killing, Jian with a new hunger for battle, Lash with a fire that never got to burn, and Kadir with the growing awareness that something below is watching.

## 1. The Council's Dilemma

The Council convened at dawn. The mood was grim — not the grim of defeat, but the grim of hard arithmetic. Gravehold had survived the first storm, but the watchtowers were reporting new threats on two fronts, and the mages could not be everywhere at once.

Elder Councillor Sera stood at the head of the table, a map of the city and its surrounding territories spread before her. Two markers had been placed on it: one at the Outer Settlements, one at the Old Foundry. She looked at each mage in turn — Adelheim, still wearing the dust of the East Gate on his cloak; Xaxos, his breaches faintly aglow even in repose; Brama, her hands raw from a night of healing; and the empty chair where Kadir should have been.

"We have one team of mages," she said. "We have two crises. Choose."

---

## 2. The Mage Pool

Gravehold is not a single battlefield — it is a sprawling city of districts, passages, and walls, and the mages within it cannot be everywhere at once. After the battle at the East Gate, the surviving mages are scattered across the city, each bound by the realities of distance, duty, and recovery.

**Adelheim** and **Xaxos** remain at the East Gate, overseeing the repair crews who are already at work on the shattered outer wall. They can reach the Outer Settlements within hours, or the Old Foundry within less. They cannot reach the western districts in time to respond to a crisis there — the city is too large, the Warrens too slow.

**Kadir** lies in the Healer's Ward, deep in the Upper Ward. Her emerald shard flickers weakly in the lamplight. The healers say she will recover, but not quickly. She is unavailable for the next mission.

**Brama** has spent the hours since the battle in the Lower Markets, tending to the fever outbreak. She is exhausted but upright, and she has made it clear she will fight if needed. She can reach the Outer Settlements or the Old Foundry, but she cannot be in two places at once.

Beyond these three, the city is full of mages who answered the Council's summons but have not yet been called to fight. They wait in the Upper Ward, in the Warrens, in the guest quarters of the Spire — restless, watching, ready.

## 3. Side Mission: The Ember in the Dark

A child found a dying drake in the Warrens near the Old Foundry — a lesser dragon, charcoal-scaled with ember-orange edges, displaced by the disturbance beneath the city. The Council is divided on whether to spend resources saving it. Elder Councillor Sera has made her position clear: "We are not the kind of city that lets things die in the dark."

## 4. The Two Threats

The Council has identified two emerging threats, and the mages can only address one:

- **Option A: The Vanished Swarm** — Smaller creatures sighted in the ash-fields are heading toward the Outer Settlements. The settlements are Gravehold's lifeline. If not defended, they will be lost.

- **Option B: The Whisper's Echo** — Kellan Voss stirred, and the black crystal pulsed in response to a signal from the sealed Deepvein Tunnels. Something is calling from below. If not investigated, the signal intensifies and Kellan's condition worsens.

*The players' choice determines Mission 2. See `CurrentMission.md` for mechanical details and `Sessions/` for the resolved outcome.*

## 5. The Mission: The Vanished Swarm

The Council chose the Outer Settlements. The swarm could not be allowed to reach the farms and homesteads that fed Gravehold. Brama, Jian, and Lash were dispatched at first light.

The Outer Settlements were a patchwork of fields, low stone walls, and scattered homesteads — nothing like the fortified walls of the East Gate. The settlers had been evacuated to root cellars and storm shelters, but the land itself was undefended. The mages arrived to find the ash-fields alive with movement: chitinous shapes skittering through the grey haze, the Carapace Queen's advance guard.

The Queen herself emerged from the ash like a nightmare given form — a vast, segmented body bristling with spines, her brood-sacs pulsing with unborn horrors. She was not here to conquer. She was here to *feed*. The Outer Settlements were a nursery to her, a place to plant her eggs and let her children devour everything that lived.

Brama took the lead, her golden light cutting through the ash-fall. She had spent the night in the fever wards, her hands still raw from healing, but there was steel beneath the weariness. She called the targets, directed the flow of battle, and when the moment came — when the rhythm of combat shifted and fortune placed the killing stroke in her hands — she did not hesitate. Two bolts of searing light, charged and waiting in her breaches. She loosed them both. The Queen's carapace shattered like glass. The swarm's heart stopped.

Jian fought with the precision of a man who had been waiting his whole life for someone to point him at a target. He reached deep into his reserves and split a single bolt of searing light into twin spears of devastation — a technique few mages ever mastered. Fulminate's energy arced from his breaches like chain lightning, each bolt striking with doubled fury. He was not flashy. He was not loud. He simply *ended* things.

Lash moved through the battle like a blade in the dark. When the Broodwomb emerged — a pulsing, organic factory that would have flooded the field with Husks — Lash was already there. She destroyed it before it could spawn a single creature. She had planned to weave a cascade of Thermal Darts, each one feeding the next, building into an inferno — but the Queen fell before the chain could ignite. She did not need it. She needed to be exactly where she was, exactly when she was there, and she was.

The battle was comprehensive. The mages never saw the Queen's deepest horrors — the brood-sacs she kept in reserve, the nightmares she had not yet birthed, the final wave of spawn that would have turned the tide. A Venomite scuttled from the ash — a bloated, venom-dripping horror — but the mages paid it no mind. The Queen was already dying, and the creature's purpose died with her. When Brama's bolts struck home, the Queen's body collapsed into the ash, her brood-sacs shrivelling, her swarm scattering into the grey.

The city's wards held strong — barely scratched by the skirmish. No mage fell. The Outer Settlements were saved.

## 6. Debrief / Aftermath

The Council convened that evening in a mood that bordered on disbelief. Two missions, two victories — and this one had been a rout. Elder Councillor Sera read the report aloud: Carapace Queen defeated, Outer Settlements intact, no casualties, no mage had pushed beyond their limits. The chamber erupted in something that was almost laughter — the nervous, relieved laughter of people who had expected to be mourning by nightfall.

But the celebration was tempered. While the mages fought in the ash-fields, the other threat had not been idle.

Kellan Voss — the miner who had emerged from the Deepvein tunnels clutching a pulsing black crystal — had suffered a seizure during the battle. The healers reported that the crystal's rhythm had intensified, its pulses growing stronger and faster, as if something in the depths was *calling* to it. Kellan survived, but he was weaker now, his speech slurred, his eyes unfocused. The crystal had begun to emit a low, constant hum that could be felt through the stone floors of the Healer's Ward.

The Deepvein signal was growing stronger. Something below was waking faster now.

The Council also noted the drake — still in the Warrens, still dying. The child who had found it had been visiting every day, bringing scraps of meat from her own meals. The scholars were doing what they could, but without proper resources, the creature's condition was deteriorating. The Council Favour that might have saved it had been held in reserve. The drake's fate remained uncertain.

## 7. Quiet Moments

### Brama

The healers' tent in the Outer Settlements was still standing. Brama had not planned to go there — she had meant to walk the perimeter, check the walls, do the things a mage was supposed to do after a battle. But her feet carried her through the tent flap before her mind caught up, and then she was washing her hands in the basin and asking the settlement's physician what she could do to help.

There were no serious injuries. A few settlers had been caught by falling debris during the evacuation; a child had twisted an ankle. Brama treated them all. She did not use magic — her reserves were spent, her breaches cold — but she had been a healer long before she had been a mage, and her hands remembered the work. She set the ankle. She cleaned the cuts. She told the child a story about a golden bird that flew through the dark to bring light to a sleeping city.

When the last patient had been seen, she sat on a crate outside the tent and looked at her hands. They were shaking. Not from exhaustion — from the memory of the searing light leaving her breaches, the Queen's carapace cracking, the sound it made. She had killed something today. Not healed. *Killed*.

She had spent the first mission in the fever wards, tending the sick while Kadir, Adelheim, and Xaxos fought Rageborne. She had told herself that her work mattered just as much — and it did, it *did* — but there had been a part of her that wondered if she was hiding. If she had chosen the wards because she was afraid of the gate.

Today she had not hidden. Today she had stood in the ash and loosed the bolts that ended a nightmare. And she had been *good* at it. That was the part that frightened her most.

She stayed in the tent until dawn, sleeping in a chair beside the child with the twisted ankle. When she woke, her hands had stopped shaking.

### Jian

Jian did not go to the healers' tent. He did not go to the celebration that the settlers were trying to organise in the village square. He walked to the edge of the ash-fields, where the grey haze met the dark soil of the farmlands, and he sat down with his back against a boundary stone.

He was not tired. He was not injured. He was *full* — full of an energy he did not know how to name. The power he had unleashed — twin bolts of devastation, Fulminate arcing between them, searing light tearing through the Queen's hide — he had never done that before. He had never even imagined doing that before. It had felt like something unlocking inside him, a door he had not known was there.

And now he did not know what to do with it.

Jian was not a man given to introspection. He was a soldier — or he had been, before the breaches chose him. He understood orders, objectives, clear lines of engagement. He did not understand the thing that was humming in his chest right now, the thing that wanted to go back out into the ash and find something else to fight.

He sat against the boundary stone until the stars came out. He did not pray. He did not meditate. He just sat, and breathed, and waited for the humming to quiet.

It did not.

### Lash

Lash found the Broodwomb's remains at the edge of the battlefield — a collapsed sack of chitin and ichor, already being picked over by ash-beetles. She stood over it for a long time.

She had killed it before it could spawn. That was the job. That was what she did. But standing there in the grey twilight, she felt something she had not expected: disappointment.

The cascade of Thermal Darts had been her plan. She had spent the journey to the Outer Settlements thinking about it — the way the darts would chain, the way the heat would build, the way she would become a furnace. But the Queen had died too quickly. The darts never linked. The furnace never lit.

She had won. She had done her job. And she felt like she had missed something.

Lash was not a woman who spoke easily about her feelings. She had grown up in the Upper Ward, the daughter of a crystal-cutter, and she had learned early that wanting things out loud was a good way to lose them. But standing over the Broodwomb's corpse, she let herself want something, just for a moment: a fight that lasted long enough for her to *burn*.

She turned away from the remains and walked back toward the settlement. Tomorrow she would be fine. Tonight she would let herself be disappointed.

### Kadir

In the Healer's Ward, Kadir opened her eyes.

She had been dreaming of green light — the same dream she had dreamed after Rageborne, the same emerald pulse that had flared from her shard as she struck the killing blow. But this time the dream was different. This time the green light was not alone. There was something else in the darkness with it — something vast and patient and *old*. It did not speak. It did not move. It simply watched her with an attention that felt like pressure, like the weight of deep water.

She woke to the sound of the black crystal humming.

Kellan Voss lay in the bed across the ward, his face pale, his breathing shallow. The crystal on his chest pulsed in a slow, steady rhythm — a heartbeat that was not his own. Kadir watched it for a long time, her own shard flickering in sympathy.

She was recovered now. The healers had cleared her for duty. But as she lay there in the dark, listening to the crystal's hum, she understood something she had not understood before: the thing below was not just a threat. It was a *presence*. And it was aware of them.

She did not sleep again that night.

### Adelheim & Xaxos

At the East Gate, Adelheim and Xaxos received the news of the victory by runner — a young woman from the settlements who had run the entire distance without stopping, her face flushed with exertion and joy. "The Queen is dead," she gasped. "The settlements are safe."

Adelheim thanked her and sent her to the mess hall for food and rest. Then he turned to Xaxos, who was standing on the repaired section of the wall, his breaches faintly aglow in the evening light.

"They didn't need us," Xaxos said. It was not a question.

"No," Adelheim said. "They didn't."

Xaxos was quiet for a moment. Then he laughed — a short, surprised sound, as if he had not expected it. "Good," he said. "That's good."

Adelheim nodded. He understood. They had held the gate against Rageborne. They had proven themselves. And now the next generation — Brama, Jian, Lash — had proven themselves too. The city was not dependent on any one mage, any one team. It was stronger than that.

They stood on the wall together, watching the stars come out over the ash-fields, and neither of them spoke. They did not need to.
